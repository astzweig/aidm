Say how AI helped build your software.

AI Development Mode gives repositories a simple, human-readable way to disclose how much agency humans and generative AI had during development, without fake percentages or line-by-line attribution.

One README badge is enough to adopt it. The helper below finds the right one.

Reference

The modes

A mode describes the typical workflow for substantive changes. It is not a quality score and no mode is better than another. Modes are listed from most to least AI autonomy.

Autonomous
AI performs substantially the complete development process after the initial goal or specification.
Supervised
AI independently handles substantial multi-step work while humans review milestones and redirect.
Collaborative
Human and AI shape decisions and implementation together through frequent feedback loops.
Directed
The human defines requirements, architecture, and approach; AI executes bounded tasks.
Assistive
The human performs core development; AI helps with debugging, tests, docs, review, and suggestions.
Incidental
AI is only used occasionally for small local tasks.
None
AI is intentionally not used at all.
Mixed
Materially different workflows are common. Use Mixed with a profile, or publish scoped declarations, instead of averaging them into a meaningless number.

Background

Why declare it

Not a score

Modes describe how work was organized, not whether the software is good, safe, or trustworthy. No mode is ranked or colored as better.

Not authorship tracking

AIDM describes the development process. It does not claim which individual lines came from a model.

Honesty is the cheap option

An accurate declaration tells contributors what to expect and lets adopters calibrate their review. Since no mode looks better than another, there is no reason to downplay AI use.

More detail when it matters

An optional .aidm.json profile describes planning, architecture, implementation, testing, review, and the largest scope AI handles alone.

About

Open by design

AIDM is vendor-neutral, MIT licensed, and created as part of Astzweig's Social Contribution efforts. The goal is a small convention that communities can improve and reuse freely. Contribute on GitHub.
